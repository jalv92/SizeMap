SizeMap / visual spec

Every liquidity heatmap shows you that 400 lots are sitting at 7440. SizeMap shows you whether they got bought or cancelled — live, on the chart, with the arithmetic.

01 The two frames

Rendered from the real pixel logic — the same log-normalised LUT and the same grammar the C indicator will use. Synthetic book, real design.

02 Why the built-in is unusable

This was measured, not asserted. NinjaTrader's own Order Flow Market Depth Map was sampled pixel by pixel: its heatmap is matplotlib.gray to within dE 0.0014, and its entire magnitude range — from "no liquidity" to the biggest wall on screen — is eight grey values.

0.8 JND per step Every magnitude step in NT8's heatmap is below the human threshold of perceptible difference. Its loudest possible reading sits at 1.25 : 1 contrast against its own chart background.

SizeMap's ramp: nine stops, +0.084 OKLab lightness per step (≈ 8.5 JND), top stop at 13.84 : 1. Monotone under protanopia and deuteranopia both — which the candles themselves are not.

03 BEACON — the ramp

Nine stops, interpolated in sRGB into a 256-entry lookup table. Strictly monotone in OKLab lightness. Zero of the 256 entries land in the red arc [349°,60°) or the green arc [110°,177°) — the hues already spent on candles — so a liquidity band can never be mistaken for a candle body. That single constraint is what disqualified the green-to-red variant outright.

Compression: colour saturates, geometry carries the overflow

Index is log(1 + size/s0) / log(1 + sCap/s0), with s0 and sCap anchored to the median and 8× the 85th percentile of the whole book, recomputed every five seconds. One doubling of size ≈ +42 LUT indices ≈ 11 JND.

When a freak 2000-lot wall appears, max-normalising would darken the entire chart at exactly the moment something interesting happened. Here the wall saturates at 255, the rest of the chart does not move a single index, and the excess is carried by the saturation mark and by the printed number. The history ring stores lot counts, never LUT indices, so the legend is true for every column on screen, not just the newest one.

04 The grammar

The honesty rule made structural: observed liquidity is solid, remembered liquidity is hollow. Not the same band at lower alpha — a different form, readable from a screenshot with no legend. A 50% stipple was tested and rejected: it optically averages ΔL −0.145, which is almost two ramp stops, so it lies about the very magnitude it claims to preserve.

Confidence rides the dash, never alpha and never hue: 8-on at ≥0.875, then 6/2, 4/4, 2/6, and below 0.25 the band is not drawn at all. Two orthogonal channels — the colour says how big it was, the dash says how sure we are it is still there — and neither corrupts the other.

The ◇ is not decoration. When the classifier cannot separate absorbed from pulled it refuses to answer, and the chart says so. A product that admits what it does not know outranks one that always has a label.

05 The touch trace — the price, without candles

NinjaTrader's bars are not required. A 25-line custom chart style whose renderer is empty makes them draw nothing, and everything that matters survives it — the price axis, auto-scaling, the last-price marker, the crosshair, Chart Trader's order lines. The Bars object still holds every value; only the painted glyph is gone.

Which is worth doing, because a candle and this heatmap disagree about time by 240 to 1. One pixel column of heat is 3.5 seconds; the candle beside it is a 60-second summary drawn as a single glyph. And because the whole raster sits behind the bars, a candle body lands squarely on the wall row being eaten — occluding the one moment the product exists to show.

So the price becomes what actually touches the liquidity: best bid and best ask, stepped, never interpolated. No diagonals — price lives on a 0.25 grid and a diagonal draws prices that never existed. No last-trade line, because every print happens at or inside the touch, so it would lie on top of a line already drawn.

ΔL 0.381 — worst case, any colour A 1 px near-white core with a 1 px ink casing on each side. The core alone fails on backgrounds lighter than L 0.835; the casing alone fails on those darker than L 0.272. Those ranges are disjoint, with a 0.56 gap — so no colour that can ever appear on this chart defeats the pair. Zero hue spent, two tokens the palette already had.

The pixel that pays for itself

The ask core sits one pixel above its quoted row, so its lower casing lands on that row's top edge — which, when the ask is parked on a confirmed wall, is already that wall's own ink groove. Same colour, so the write does nothing. The trace costs the wall it is eating zero heat pixels, right through the absorption.

Folding, and the word for what you are seeing

When 14 quarter-second quotes fold into one pixel column, the trace draws the min and max of that column, never the mean — a mean invents a price that was never quoted. Line thickness then reads as volatility for free. But it stops being the spread and becomes the extent, so the HUD switches from SPRD to EXT and says which one you are looking at.

History, and the trap in it

Depth still has no past — the heat begins at the REC rule and that never changes. Price does: with Tick Replay on, the inside quote at every historical trade is available, so the trace covers the whole chart. It is drawn dashed, uncased, on ink ground, because a quote sampled at trades is a different observation than a book watched continuously, and the grammar already says solid means observed, dashed means reconstructed.

The trap: when a feed carries no bid/ask alongside its tick data, NinjaTrader fabricates one — bid = last, ask = bid + 1 tick. Rendered naively that is a flawless, perfectly constant one-tick spread that looks like the best data in the file. SizeMap counts it and, if more than 99% of historical spreads are exactly one tick while the live spread varies, collapses to a single line and prints Q:SYN. One honest line beats two beautiful lies.

06 The ledger — the whole product

In the blank margin right of the last bar, 44 px wide, vertically centred on the wall's band. Three segments. A 620-lot ask wall now showing 160, of the 460 that disappeared 400 were actually traded:

REMAINING
BOUGHT
CANCEL
still resting — the wall's own heat colour traded away — real demand cancelled — the seller walked

The same wall, same 620→160 collapse, but mostly cancelled, is a bar that is almost entirely grey. On every competing product those two situations look identical: a red band getting dimmer. That difference is the entire reason this project exists.

And it discloses its own bias. Trade attribution reads from a pruned ring, so a wall older than the retention window has had the trades that ate it forgotten — "traded" becomes a lower bound. When that happens the ledger's right border pixel is omitted, leaving the track visibly open. An open edge means "there was more, we lost it."

07 Deliberately not drawn

  • Blur, glow, featheringThe data is one tick by 250 ms. Anything smoother is a claim about resolution the feed cannot support.
  • Animation of any kindNT8 caps chart repaint at 250 ms. A pulse is a stutter.
  • Alpha as a data channelAlpha carries 0xFF and nothing else. Magnitude rides the LUT, confidence rides the dash.
  • Bid/ask huePosition encodes it inside the window; a 3-pixel side tick encodes it outside. Two hues would cost half the magnitude budget.
  • Bookmap's second hot rampMeasured: a −0.13 lightness drop at the seam. A big orange wall renders darker than a medium white one.
  • Callout boxes, side panels, summary panels~28% of the plot area and a collision solver, to restate what the heat and the ledger already say.
  • An in-chart DOM ladderNT8 ships a SuperDOM. Duplicating it costs 20% of the chart width for data one keystroke away.
  • Historical depth backfillDepth never fires on historical bars. The REC rule discloses where the record starts; faking it would make every other honesty rule worthless. Historical price is drawn — dashed, on ink ground, as a different observation class.
  • A spread fillThe rows between bid and ask hold no resting size, and ramp stop 0 already means exactly that. Painting them would re-encode what the ramp says and spend the one dark value reserved for structure.
  • Trade bubblesEvery print happens at or inside the touch, so every bubble would land on the trace — which already says "traded here", continuously, with no density artefact.
  • Any licence serverRuns offline, no token, no phone-home. The benchmark product stops working when its backend does.

08 Only you can answer these

  • Q1Stop 5 is a khaki and it is on screen constantly. Warm, or dirty? It cannot rotate further from green — if it bothers you the fix is chroma, not hue. Judge it after a full session, not in ten minutes.
  • Q2Hollow bands at 10 levels. With fifteen-plus on screen, does it read as a liquidity map or as scaffolding? This is the single biggest open risk in the design and no amount of specification settles it.
  • Q3The ink groove. At your monitor's gamma, is one pixel above and below a bright band enough to say "this is a confirmed object"? If not it goes to two, before anything gets a colour.
  • Q4Everything is behind the candles — including the HUD and the legend. That is the price of SetZOrder(-1) and it is all-or-nothing. If a candle lands on your legend often enough to annoy you, the fix is a second thin indicator at default z-order.
  • Q5Rose against grey at 5 px tall. They are the same lightness by design and differ only in chroma. Confirm that survives on your screen — if it does not, the ledger gets taller; the colours do not change.
  • Q6Trade one session with the candles already invisible. Three colour pickers set to Transparent in Data Series — no install, reversible in ten seconds. If you catch yourself squinting for a wick or a session open, candles stay and the trace ships off by default. Nothing else settles it.
